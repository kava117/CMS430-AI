1. ReLU is preferrable for large neural networks because it helps to avoid the vanishing gradient
problem. As blame is tracked further and further back through a network during backpropagation,
the derivatives become very small and less useful. ReLU either keeps nodes active, as is, or
changes negative nodes to 0. This can become an issue if enough nodes "die" this way, so
leaky ReLU attempts to fix the issue by returning small negative values instead of 0 so
the nodes are not completely inactivated.

2. The ReLU graph reached a higher accuracy much faster than the non-ReLU graph, but it seems
to behave more consistenly erratically. After running the program with less epochs (100 rather 
than 500), there was not nearly as much variance, leading me to believe that the eccentricity 
was due to the model's dying nodes.
